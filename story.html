Chapter 1 — “The Sign Job”

Sept 28, 2026

The rain on Neon Row fell sideways, hissing where it struck the hot vents. Echo stood under Madame Lin's awning, watching her noodle bar's great dragon sign stutter — one moment a roaring emerald beast, the next a dying ember. “Third time this month, little spark,” Madame Lin sighed, pressing a chit for double noodles into his paw.

Echo grinned his toothy grin and shrugged into his oilskin cloak, the hood settling over his copper-plated ears. Tool belt: magnetized. Backpack: humming. He scaled the rusted scaffolding with the ease of a cat half his size, rain beading on his whiskers and rolling off his armor in glowing rivulets.

Halfway up, the wind tried to take him. He hooked his tail — copper rings clinking — around a strut and kept climbing. The sign's junction box was a rat's nest of corroded wire. Echo's claws made short work of it: splicing, soldering, humming an old street tune while green sparks danced.

Then — ROAR. The dragon blazed back to life, emerald fire washing the wet street, and the whole Row cheered. Echo slid down the scaffolding, landed in a puddle with a splash, and bowed. Madame Lin pressed the noodle chit into his paw, plus one extra “for the show.”

But as he shook the rain from his cloak, his wrist scanner chirped — a signal he'd never seen before, pulsing from deep beneath the Row, in a rhythm almost like a heartbeat…

Chapter 2 — “Beneath the Row”

Sept 29, 2026

Echo couldn't sleep. The heartbeat signal kept pulsing on his wrist scanner all night, and a cat with a mystery is a cat with no rest. By dawn the rain had quit, and he'd traded the oilskin cloak for a lighter rig: a hooded utility jacket covered in glowing circuit patches, and a lamp clipped to his copper head-plate. He pried open the rusted service hatch in the noodle-bar alley and dropped into the dark.

The old service tunnels ran under Neon Row like roots under a tree — brick arches, flooded tracks, and cables thick as pythons. Echo sloshed through ankle-deep water, his head-lamp cutting the dark, the copper rings on his tail clicking as it twitched with excitement. The heartbeat grew louder on the scanner with every step, and the green circuit lines on his armor seemed to pulse in time with it.

He found it in a collapsed utility junction: a machine half-buried in silt, older than the Row itself, its riveted hull humming. At its center, a glass pod glowed faintly — and inside it slept a tiny mechanical bird, its brass wings folded, its chest rising and falling in the exact rhythm of the signal. Echo's whiskers quivered. He reached for his toolkit.

The bird's eyes snapped open — two bright pinpricks of amber — and the tunnel went very, very quiet. From the far dark came a soft, answering chirp… and something large shifted in the water ahead.

Later…

Echo pressed himself flat against the brick arch as the water rippled toward him. From the dark emerged the bird's mate — a mechanical crow three times bigger, rusted but regal, carrying a broken copper feather in its beak like an offering. The little bird in the pod chirped again, weaker now, and Echo's paw hovered over his toolkit. “Hold on, little spark,” he whispered, fitting the copper feather into the pod's slot — and the machine's heartbeat steadied into something that sounded, unmistakably, like relief.

Chapter 3 — “The Scrap Gardens”

Sept 30, 2026

Morning came with pale sun through the canal fog, and Echo surfaced from the tunnels with a brass mechanical bird tucked carefully in his pack — and a shopping list. The little bird needed a resonance coil, the crow in the dark needed convincing, and Echo needed parts older than half the junk in the city. There was only one place to go: the Scrap Gardens, the mile-wide salvage yards past the old canal locks where everything Neon Row ever threw away came to rust in the light.

He dressed for bargaining: a patched red scarf knotted at his neck, a sleeveless canvas duster over his tech-vest, and his measuring goggles pushed up on his copper brow-plate. The yards stretched out in mountains of twisted hulls and dead neon, vendors shouting over clanking conveyor lines, and the smell of hot metal and rain.

He found the coil in a vendor's brass-and-copper stall — a resonance coil, still warm, humming in a key Echo didn't recognize. The price was steep: not chits, but a favor. “The yard crawler's been down a week,” the vendor said, jerking a thumb at a hulking salvage crane. “Nobody can read its old panel code. You fix it, the coil's yours.” Echo looked at the crawler, looked at his toolkit, and grinned. Nobody ever said no to a machine.

The panel code was older than the tunnels, a language of blink-patterns and brass switches. Echo worked in the yard's dust for an hour, goggles down, tail-ring clicking against the chassis with each crouch and stretch. On the last switch, the crawler shuddered, roared, and stood — and the whole stall cheered. The vendor pressed the coil into Echo's paw, plus a brass gear “for luck.”

But as Echo tucked the coil away, it chimed against the little bird's pod — and answered. The bird's heartbeat changed, aligning to the coil's strange key… a key that matched, exactly, the heartbeat signal he'd first heard under the Row.

Later…

By dusk Echo was back at the Row, the resonance coil warm in his paw as he climbed down to the old junction. The crow waited at the dark's edge, regal as ever, and stepped aside as Echo fitted the coil into the pod's slot. The little bird's wings unfolded with a soft brass whir — and on his wrist scanner, three heartbeats pulsed in perfect, unmistakable harmony.

Chapter 4 — “The Relay Spire”

Oct 1, 2026

Echo woke at false dawn with a question buzzing louder than his alarm: where was the heartbeat coming from? The pod, the crow, and his own repaired bird now pulsed as one, but a signal has a source, and a cat with a mystery is a cat with a plan. He packed light, dressed for the heights — a snug wind-rig over his vest, a climbing harness cinched around his copper shin-plates, wind-goggles strapped to his brow-plate, and the little brass bird riding in his open breast pocket where it could see.

The Relay Spire rose from the oldest rooftop on the canal side, a skeleton of brass and glass left over from when Neon Row talked to the sky instead of the ground. Echo climbed the service ribs hand over hand, tail-ring clicking against the struts, the city falling away beneath him into morning fog. Up here the air was cold and clean, and the rooftops looked like islands floating in a white sea. The little bird chirped softly, its brass wings catching the first pink light.

At the top, in a cage of lightning-rods, he found the beacon: a brass-and-glass lantern the size of a steamer trunk, its lens dark for years — until he stepped close. The little bird flapped once, the beacon's filaments woke with a warm amber glow, and his wrist scanner filled with a voice: crackling, amused, old. “If you're hearing this, little spark, the birds found their way home. The coil is charged. Take them to the Bell.”

Echo stared at the scanner, grin slowly widening. “The Bell? There's no bell on the Row.” But the beacon was already turning, its lens flaring toward the far edge of the city — toward the old docks, where the fog glowed faintly green, and something tall and round was ringing, very faintly, in a rhythm that matched everything.

Later…

The docks were closer than they looked and stranger than he'd hoped. Echo picked his way past rope-coils and barnacled hulls as the fog thinned, and there it rose from the harbor: a bell tower built from a wrecked ship's keel, its great brass bell swinging in a wind that wasn't there. The three heartbeats on his wrist scanner fell into a fourth, deeper rhythm — the Bell's — and the little bird in his pocket folded its wings, suddenly very still, like a patient finally reaching the doctor.

Chapter 5 is being written. The Bell is ringing. Echo is already on his way.
