World · teaser

Neon Row

A rain-slicked market street in a vertical city that never fully dries. Old brick and rusted scaffolding below, neon and steam above. It runs on repaired things — nothing here is new, everything is maintained, and the people who keep it running are its real citizens. Warm noir. Danger exists, but community is stronger.

The Row's people pay in kind: chits, favors, food. A fixed sign is worth double noodles and one extra for the show. A working crawler is worth a resonance coil and a brass gear for luck. Keep the street economy, and the street keeps you.

Places

Madame Lin's noodle bar

Ground floor. The great emerald dragon sign over the door — repaired by Echo, twice running and counting. The awning out front is neutral ground, where news travels faster than rain.

The service tunnels

Brick arches, flooded tracks, cables thick as pythons, running under the Row like roots under a tree. Older than the street above. Something down there is breathing in rhythm.

The Scrap Gardens

Mile-wide salvage yards past the old canal locks, where everything the Row ever threw away comes to rust in the light. If a part exists, it's here — for the right favor.

The Relay Spire

A skeleton of brass and glass on the oldest rooftop, left over from when the Row talked to the sky instead of the ground. Its beacon woke up recently. Nobody knows why.

The old docks

Past the fog line, where the harbor glows faintly green. A bell tower built from a wrecked ship's keel. Its bell swings in a wind that isn't there.

People & creatures

Echo

Cyberpunk tinkerer. Orange cat, black tiger stripes, copper-and-bronze armor with glowing circuit lines, olive tech-vest crowded with gadgets. Fixes things, can't leave a mystery alone.

Madame Lin

Runs the noodle bar. Dry, warm, observant. Pays double chits for good work. Calls Echo “little spark.” Knows more about the Row's history than she lets on.

The mechanical bird

Tiny, brass wings, amber eyes. Slept in a glass pod under the street, its chest rising and falling in the exact rhythm of the heartbeat signal. Weak, faltering — then repaired.

The crow

The bird's mate. Three times bigger, rusted but regal. Carried a broken copper feather like an offering. It answered from the far dark — which means there are more of them down there.

Open questions

The Row keeps its secrets the way it keeps its machines: maintained, not explained.

The heartbeat. It pulsed from beneath the street before Echo ever went down. It matched the bird's breathing. After the feather, it steadied into something like relief. What is the machine, and why is it older than the Row?

The makers. Someone manufactured mechanical animals and buried the machine under the street. Who — and why were they abandoned?

The answering chirp. Something large shifted in the water. The crow came from the far dark. There is a population down there. Are they all failing like the little bird was?

The voice in the beacon. Crackling, amused, old: “Take them to the Bell.” Who left a message in a dead lantern, and how long has it been waiting?

Mysteries open faster than they close around here. Never more than one answered at a time — and always a new question waiting at the docks.
